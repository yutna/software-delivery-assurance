# v1.1.0 template release announcement

**Status:** final for the v1.1.0 template release on 2026-10-28.
**Content version:** SRAS 1.1.0. **Release:** v1.1.0 template release.

This is a **template release**, not a standard release. The announcement uses
verified repository statements. It does not name a customer, a real project, a
reviewer, a date that has not happened, or a sign-off that does not exist.

## Short announcement

> The Software Delivery Assurance project has published the 1.1.0 template
> release of its SRAS starter kit. The release shows rationale, assessment
> guidance, references, and crosswalk links on generated requirement pages,
> adds a targeted NIST SP 800-218 (SSDF) crosswalk, documents private CI
> validation and 1.x upgrades for adopters, and makes an unsigned sign-off
> explicit. The kit provides 167 active requirements across a Core, five
> technical modules, and four Thailand sector overlays, plus 27 regulatory
> register entries, eight informative crosswalks, a project-instance CLI,
> deterministic reference pages, and a content guard. It is usable with
> limitations. The Thai translation is machine-assisted and partial, the
> registers are unverified, and no external or human review is claimed. The kit
> is not a standard, not a certification, not legal advice, and not evidence of
> compliance.

## Who it is for

- Thai government digital teams, state enterprises, and vendors delivering
  public services.
- Product owners, engineering leads, security and privacy teams, operations
  teams, assessors, and bid teams.
- Finance, e-commerce, and logistics teams that need the same evidence
  discipline.
- Teams already running a 1.0.0 instance that need the upgrade path.

## What the release contains

- A copyable project template and a private project-instance workflow.
- The SRAS Core with 70 active requirements.
- Five technical modules with 52 requirements: AI/GenAI, integration and API,
  regulated data, cloud/SaaS, and on-premises.
- Four Thailand sector overlays with 45 deltas: finance, e-commerce, logistics,
  and public sector.
- 27 regulatory register entries: 12 Thailand, 6 ASEAN, and 9 EU.
- Eight informative crosswalks, including the new NIST SP 800-218 (SSDF) 1.1
  crosswalk.
- Generated requirement pages with rationale, lite and full assessment
  guidance, external references, and crosswalk links.
- An adopter operations guide for private CI validation, 1.x upgrades, and the
  synthetic end-to-end example, with a supported-platform statement.
- An explicit `Not signed` sentinel for a fresh sign-off and a non-fatal
  validation warning.
- A TypeScript CLI for `init`, `project validate`, `project refresh`,
  `project resolve`, `render`, and `guard`.
- A bilingual English and Thai documentation site.
- Deterministic reference pages and a content guard.

## What changed since 1.0.0

- Requirement pages and generated crosswalk pages show the full assessment
  material.
- The SSDF crosswalk records the practices it does not cover.
- `check-version` checks both `package-lock.json` version fields.
- Issue forms ask for scope, acceptance criteria, verification, and out-of-scope
  where the contribution workflow expects them.
- `CITATION.cff` carries software citation metadata.
- No requirement ID, schema shape, or gate semantic changed.

## What it does not claim

- It is not a certification, accreditation, audit attestation, or regulatory
  approval.
- It is not legal advice or a definitive statement of legal compliance.
- It is not a published standard with external sign-off.
- No external human reviewer has been engaged. The AI audit is not independent
  and is not a substitute for external review.
- The Thai translation is `translation_status: partial` with
  `machine_translated: true`. No named human Thai reviewer has confirmed it.
- The 27 register entries are `review_status: unverified`.
- The four legal concerns are desk research notes, not legal opinions.
- The template is generic. It does not encode agency-specific procurement,
  records, or approval rules.
